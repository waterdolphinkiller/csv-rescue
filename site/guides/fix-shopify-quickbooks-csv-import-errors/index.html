How to Fix Shopify, HubSpot & QuickBooks CSV Import Errors in Under 2 Minutes

Direct Answer: Most Shopify, HubSpot, and QuickBooks CSV import failures come from five structural bugs: unquoted commas inside US street addresses shifting columns, unclosed quotes swallowing rows, mixed currency formats ($1,250.50 vs ($45.00)), ambiguous MM/DD/YYYY vs DD/MM/YYYY dates, and duplicate rows. CSV Rescue isolates broken rows in an Uncertainty Queue and fixes deterministic issues with Before/After previews.

The 4:55 PM Friday Import Rejection Wall

You are five minutes from closing the week. You upload your vendor order sheet to Shopify or QuickBooks, and the importer throws a cryptic message: "Line 47: Expected 9 columns, got 10" or "Invalid amount format on row 184."

Digging through a 30,000-row CSV in Notepad to find one missing quote or one unquoted comma in "742 Evergreen Ave, Boston MA" is brutal—and blindly running find-and-replace on commas will destroy the rest of the file.

Step-by-Step Local Repair Workflow

  1. Drop your failing CSV into CSV Rescue—or click "Broken Orders CSV" on the home screen to see a live US e-commerce example immediately.
  2. Review the Health Report: critical structural errors (ROW_WIDTH_MISMATCH, QUOTE_ERROR) are separated from safe fixes (blank rows, repeated headers, extra spaces).
  3. Click "Apply Safe Fixes Only" to clean deterministic noise in one click, then confirm currency normalization and MDY/DMY date order in Review Fixes.
  4. Open the Uncertainty Queue to accept, manually edit, or separately export the handful of genuinely broken rows—so 99.8% of your clean orders import on time.
Honest boundary: ambiguous dates like 04/03/2026 cannot be guessed mathematically (it could be April 3 in the US or March 4 in the UK/MY). CSV Rescue always asks you to confirm MDY vs DMY before converting to ISO YYYY-MM-DD.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does one unclosed quote break thousands of rows in standard CSV parsers?

Strict RFC 4180 parsers keep consuming newlines until they find a matching quote. CSV Rescue bounds multiline lookahead and isolates unclosed quotes on their exact source line so subsequent records stay intact.

Does CSV Rescue handle QuickBooks accounting negative amounts like ($45.00) or 45.00-?

Yes. The normalize_currency rule recognizes parenthesized negatives, trailing minus signs, commas, and US/international currency symbols, converting them to standard numeric decimals like -45.00.

Can I map messy vendor headers like "Item Desc" and "Unit Price" to a standard schema?
